A pallet pays rent before it pays you

The return pallet rankings sort lots by take-home per hour, and every ledger stops at the sort table: manifest price, realization rate, eleven hours of unboxing, an hourly figure. The floor under the sort table never shows up. A pallet occupies a slot, and a slot carries rent the way a truck carries fuel. The rent is small per pallet and it compounds with every week the lot sits unsold, and it lands entirely on the dollar side of the hourly math because rent consumes no labor hours at all.

Take a 10x10 storage unit at $90 a month holding twelve pallets. A slot runs $7.50 per pallet-month. A $650 manifest pallet that clears inside its first month carried $7.50 of rent; the same pallet stuck for a full quarter carried $22.50. At a 45 percent realization the recovery is $292.50, so the fast lot nets $285.00 and the slow one $270.00. Across eleven sort hours that is $25.91 an hour against $24.55. Nothing about the work changed. The sort took the same skill, the listing photos the same effort, and the quoted hourly figure on a ranking page would read identically for both.

The slot, not the pallet, is the scarce asset, and turnover is how it pays. A slot cycled every four weeks turns thirteen pallets a year; twelve slots move 156 pallets and divide the unit's $1,080 annual bill into $6.92 of rent each. Let every lot sit two months and the same twelve slots turn 52 pallets, so the identical $1,080 arrives to $20.77 per pallet. Total rent paid is fixed. Slow operators do not pay less rent, they pay the same rent fewer times over, which is why the small boxed goods sit above furniture in any honest ranking: boxed goods clear a slot in weeks and furniture parks in one for a season.

Two moves price the slot back in your favor. Shelving at $120 turns one floor slot into two rack slots for $2 a month across a five-year life, dropping the effective slot to $3.75 and cutting every rent figure above in half. And the clearance rule writes itself from the arithmetic: a pallet that has stopped moving is paying $7.50 a month for the privilege of waiting, so its residual value competes against the slot it blocks. Moving a stalled lot at a small loss can beat holding it, because the freed slot takes a fresh $650 manifest at $292.50 of recovery.
